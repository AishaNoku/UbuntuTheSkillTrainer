Lesson 1: What Is Crocheting?

What is Crocheting?

Crocheting is a fiber art technique that uses a single hook and yarn to create fabric through a series of loops and stitches. Unlike knitting—which uses two needles—crocheting relies on one tool: the crochet hook. This makes it simple to learn and beginner-friendly. Crochet has been around for centuries and is still one of the most popular yarn crafts today. It's not difficult to learn how to crochet, but it can be a little tricky in the beginning. Like learning any new skill, mastering crochet will take a fair bit of perseverance and patience. To learn crochet, start with the fundamental techniques and a few basic stitches. Then, once you've mastered them, you can move on to intermediate and advanced techniques. Once you've learned the basics of crochet, you can make all sorts of cozy crocheted items, from baby booties to granny squares.

A Brief History of Crocheting

The origins of crochet as we know it today, is more recent than you might expect! Evidence shows it originated as its own craft in the mid-1800s. Crocheted edging was often used to decorate lace pieces in 18th & 19th century (shown in many items housed at the Victoria and Albert Museum.) The earliest known published crochet pattern was in a Dutch magazine called Penélopé, in 1823. It listed instructions and systems for five types of purses. The first patterns for crocheted bags were published in Germany and The Netherlands in magazines in 1835. By this time, patterns introduced two more stitches to create a variance in the single and double crochet designs. Yet crochet's family tree is full of fascinating ancestors! Here are some who no doubt made their mark… It's hard to narrow down the exact origins and who invented it. The most reliable and oldest link to crochet is a unique Chinese embroidery technique or the French method of tambouring. Tambour lace was a lace fabric technique popular in 18th century. It was where you stretched fine netting over a frame, and used a tiny, pointed hook to create chain stitches to form patterns over the netting.

  • Europe — Used as lacework for wealthy households.
  • China & Middle East — Early forms of needle looping.
  • Ireland — Irish crochet lace became a major source of income during the Irish famine.

People only started using slip stitch crochet to refer to the craft in the mid-1800s. It was only later it became known as just crochet. Crochet comes from the Old French as a diminutive of croche, which in term is derived from the Germanic word croc, meaning small hook. (The word also has roots in crochetage, meaning a single stitch used to join separate bits of lace together, used in French lacemaking in the 1600s.)

Purpose of Crocheting

Crocheting is more than just a hobby; it's a valuable life skill with numerous benefits. From improving mental health to fostering a sense of community, this craft offers a range of advantages that make it worthwhile for people of all ages. Whether you're looking to reduce stress, develop fine motor skills, or even turn your passion into a business, crocheting has something to offer.

Crocheting serves many purposes, including:

  • Creating clothing like sweaters, hats, scarves, and tops
  • Making blankets, bags, accessories, and home décor
  • Therapeutic stress relief and mindfulness
  • Exploring creativity through handmade designs
  • Building a creative hobby or small business

In summary: Crocheting is a relaxing, creative, and practical skill that allows you to turn yarn into beautiful handmade items.
